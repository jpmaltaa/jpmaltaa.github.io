I run a collegiate esports organization with more than 120 players across 15 game divisions, and I have been part of it since 2023 — first as a Rocket League player, then as a division director, and since 2025 as its president.

PUCCAMP Cardinals was founded in 2016 and won its first title in 2017. Today it fields teams in League of Legends, CS2, Valorant, Rocket League, Rainbow Six, EA FC and a dozen other games, runs a women's league called Canaries, and competes out of a dedicated gaming arena on campus that opened in October 2025.

My day job is in technology — I work as a junior analyst in technological innovation at a legal tech company, and I finish my Information Systems degree in December 2026. That background is why I run the organization the way I do: with processes, documentation and data, not just enthusiasm. What I want next is the management education and the international network to do this professionally.

What I have built as president

  • A gaming arena on campus

    Opened in October 2025 and now the home venue for training, tryouts and in-person tournaments.

  • A partnership with Red Bull Brazil

    Brand partnership supporting the organization's competitive and community activity.

  • Canaries, a women's league

    Dedicated Valorant and League of Legends rosters, created to open a route into competition that the main pipeline was not reaching.

  • A sponsorship media kit

    Priced inventory for jersey placements, sold directly to local sponsors — the organization's own revenue, alongside merchandise.

  • An institutional handbook

    Written governance: mission, leadership structure, staff areas, member journey, annual calendar, code of conduct and succession rules.

  • A restructuring plan for 2026—27

    A two-year plan to professionalize the organization — defined roles, playbooks for division directors, a content pipeline, and a presidential succession process. The stated goal is an organization that runs without depending on its president.
